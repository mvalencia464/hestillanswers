@@ EX01 | story | night | road | True story · Recorded 1885
H: NINE O'CLOCK. NO MOON.|HE DROPPED THE REINS|AND ASKED GOD TO|*GUIDE THE HORSE.*
C: A student preacher had promised a little country church he would be there on Sunday. The train ran off the track. By the time the wreck was cleared, his connection was gone.

Friends told him to stay. It was forty miles of country he did not know, the roads were heavy from rain, and it was already night.

He hired a horse known for endurance and set out at nine. He could find no one who knew the exact road. So he turned the horse toward the village and prayed, "O God, starting out to preach thy word to-morrow, direct the way. Guide this horse."

At every crossroad he dropped the lines over the dashboard and prayed the same prayer. When the horse chose a road, he urged him on.

As day began to break they came out of a wood onto a road almost nobody used, and rode into the village he was looking for. The largest congregation of the summer was waiting.

The next day, in full daylight, with people directing him again and again, he lost the way and went seven miles out of his course.

"Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path."

What road are you on tonight that you cannot see the end of?

@@ EX02 | story | dawn | cabin | True story · Bristol
H: AFTER BREAKFAST|THERE WAS NOTHING|LEFT FOR DINNER.|*HE TOLD NO ONE.*
C: George Muller kept the orphan houses in Bristol by prayer alone. He did not publish what they lacked. He did not write to donors when the cupboard was empty.

He said it himself: "Greater and more manifest nearness of the Lord's presence I have never had, than when after breakfast, there were no means for dinner, and then the Lord provided the dinner for more than one hundred persons; and when after dinner, there were no means for the tea; and yet the Lord provided the tea; and all this without one single human being having been informed about our need."

"Give us this day our daily bread" was not a line for Sunday. It was how the next meal arrived.

The loaf on the table was not a surplus. It was the meal that was not there an hour before.

What is on your table that you have stopped calling an answer?
R: HX | George Muller's own account of the Bristol orphan houses, as printed in The Wonders of Prayer (1885).
